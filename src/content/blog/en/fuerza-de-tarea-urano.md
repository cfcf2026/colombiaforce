The President of Colombia announced the activation of **Joint Task Force Urano**, created to go after the entire hydrocarbon-theft chain: seizure, storage, transport, purchase and sale. He said the General Command of the Armed Forces activated it a few days ago. It brings together units from the Army, Navy, Air Force and National Police, working with prosecutors from the Attorney General's Office and intelligence and criminal-investigation teams.

## What the government ordered

The president said the state's response should not be limited to the place where the theft happens, and that authorities will follow the money to identify those who finance, transport, store and buy the fuel. He stated that fuel theft funds illegal armed groups and, in his view, those involved are part of the terrorist chain.

## First results

According to figures presented by the government:

- **Since September 1:** more than 100,000 gallons recovered, 21 arrests for hydrocarbon-related offenses, 22 vehicles seized and 46 illegal taps found.
- **Losses:** 3.7 million fewer gallons than the previous month, a 48% drop.
- **Economic impact** on the illegal trade: about 58 billion pesos.
- **Context for the year:** more than 1.2 million barrels reported stolen, worth over 771 billion pesos.

The president said these are good results but "not enough," and that the task force will have fulfilled its mission when the criminal business is no longer viable.

*Figures are those reported by the government and have not been independently verified. People who have been arrested are presumed innocent until a judge rules otherwise.*
