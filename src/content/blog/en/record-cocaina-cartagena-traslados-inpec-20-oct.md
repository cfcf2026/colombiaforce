Colombia's National Police seized 7.6 tons of cocaine at the port of Cartagena, the largest seizure recorded at that terminal in the last decade, President Abelardo De La Espriella confirmed. The shipment was bound for the Netherlands.

![Cocaine shipment seized at the port of Cartagena](/images/cocaine1.png)

## How the drugs were hidden

The cocaine — 7 tons, 646 kilos and 400 grams, to be exact — was mixed with powdered panela (raw cane sugar), a product Colombia exports year-round. It's a concealment method authorities already recognize: the texture and color of powdered panela make it hard to visually distinguish from the drug during cargo inspections. Police attributed the find to cargo profiling under Strategy Esmeralda, a port-intelligence program the government has used in other recent strikes on Caribbean maritime routes.

![Cocaine seized mixed with powdered panela, port of Cartagena](/images/cocaine2.png)

The U.S. Embassy in Colombia said the operation drew on weeks of intelligence sharing with the DEA.

## The number in context

According to the president, in 75 days in office the security forces have seized 132 tons of cocaine, valued at $6.108 billion. This specific shipment would be worth more than $200 million on the European market, compared to roughly 43 billion pesos in Colombia — a gap that illustrates why the Colombian Caribbean remains a priority route for transnational trafficking toward Europe.

**On the Colombia Force map:** this is the second major maritime seizure we've covered in the Caribbean region in under a week, following the 3,688 kg intercepted off Cartagena on October 17 alongside the strike on dissidents in Puerto Rico, Meta.

## Third wave of prison transfers since August

![Inmate transfer carried out by Colombia's prison agency](/images/prison.png)

The same day, Colombia's prison agency (INPEC) transferred 91 inmates to other facilities: 41 from Montería, 46 from Sincelejo, 2 from Cartagena and 2 from Barranquilla. The president said the move was necessary because prisons cannot keep functioning as command centers for criminal structures.

This isn't the first such wave: on his very first day in office, August 7, the government transferred 117 high-profile inmates from La Paz prison in Itagüí, and on August 25 it moved another 20 inmates from Barranquilla to Bogotá. Including today's, at least 228 prison transfers have been reported in the government's first 75 days, part of a strategy that also includes a still-under-study plan to hold inmates on Navy vessels (REMAS).

In Girón, Santander — one of the frequent destinations for these transfers — INPEC, working with the National Police's GAULA unit, seized a SIM card, 5.58 grams of a plant substance, a battery and a data cable inside a prison facility — items typically used to coordinate criminal activity from behind bars.
